# AI tells: patterns that make prose sound machine-written

A checklist for `draft-review` (and for drafting in `writing-style`). Each
pattern is something a reader notices, often without being able to say why.
One instance is rarely fatal; a cluster is. Fix by saying the thing plainly,
in the author's voice, or by cutting it.

## Vocabulary

Words and phrases that read as filler or as a model reaching for "writerly":

- delve, dive into, deep dive (as a verb), unpack, navigate (non-literal),
  leverage (as a verb for "use"), utilize, harness, unlock, empower, elevate,
  streamline, seamless(ly), robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize
- tapestry, landscape, realm, ecosystem (when not literal), journey, testament
- "It's worth noting that", "It's important to note", "Notably",
  "Interestingly", "Importantly"
- "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving world of",
  "In the realm of"
- "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "Without further ado"
- "This is where X comes in", "Enter X."
- "a powerful tool", "plays a crucial role", "serves as a reminder"

## Structure and rhythm

- **Uniform sentence length.** Paragraphs where every sentence is 15–25
  words. Real writing mixes a four-word sentence with a long one.
- **Rule of three.** Lists of exactly three adjectives or clauses, used
  reflexively ("fast, reliable, and scalable"). Use two, or one, or four if
  that's the truth.
- **"Not X, but Y" / "It's not just X, it's Y".** A contrast set up only to be
  knocked down. State Y.
- **Em-dash habit.** More than one or two em-dashes in a post. Use a comma,
  colon, parentheses, or a new sentence.
- **Colon-reveal headings and titles.** "Unity Catalog: The Key to
  Governance". Name the actual point.
- **Generic headings.** "Introduction", "Overview", "Key Takeaways",
  "Conclusion", "Final Thoughts", "Wrapping Up".
- **Bullet soup.** Lists where prose would read better, or every paragraph
  turned into bullets with bolded lead-ins ("**Speed:** It's fast.").
- **Signposting.** "First, let's look at…", "Now that we've covered X, let's
  move on to Y", "As mentioned above".

## Openings and closings

- **Throat-clearing opener.** A paragraph of context before the point
  ("Data engineering has changed a lot in recent years…"). Start with the
  problem, the moment, or the take.
- **Faux-enthusiasm opener.** "Have you ever wondered…?", "Picture this:",
  "Imagine a world where…".
- **Recap closer.** A final section that repeats the post ("In summary, we
  learned…"). End on the last useful thing, a next step, or a genuine
  open question.
- **Inspirational sign-off.** "The future is bright", "Happy coding!", "The
  possibilities are endless".

## Stance

- **Empty hedges.** "may potentially", "could arguably", "it depends on your
  use case" with no follow-up. Either commit, or say exactly what it depends
  on and why you're unsure.
- **Both-sidesing a take.** Presenting every option as equally valid when the
  author has an opinion. Say which one you'd pick.
- **Unearned superlatives.** "the best", "incredibly powerful", "truly
  transformative" with no evidence.
- **Vendor voice.** Product names in every sentence, features listed as
  benefits, nothing that went wrong. Practitioners mention the rough edges.
- **Fake specificity.** Invented numbers, benchmarks, quotes, or anecdotes.
  Every number needs a source or the author's own measurement.

## Formatting

- Bold sprinkled on random phrases mid-sentence.
- Emoji as bullet markers or in headings.
- Title Case Headings On Everything (use sentence case).
- Exclamation marks to manufacture excitement.
